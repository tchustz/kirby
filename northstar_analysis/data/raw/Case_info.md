# Care conversation standard

Northstar's one-page framework for upset customers is called CLEAR. Team Leads have used it in huddles for two years. It is a conversational guide, not a script to recite in order.

1. **Connect:** acknowledge the practical impact in the customer's words. Avoid generic apologies.
2. **Listen:** get just enough detail to identify the issue and its effect.
3. **Explain:** tell the customer what you know, what you do not know, and the next action in plain language.
4. **Act:** take the authorized step or make a complete handoff.
5. **Recap:** confirm what happened, what comes next, and when the customer should expect it.

The framework was designed for varied calls, so it does not identify product-specific eligibility, remedies, or systems. The last refresher module used a generic damaged-package example. Quality reviewers report that many Guides can name CLEAR but do not consistently move from acknowledgment to the right action on complex product issues. Completion of the refresher is not evidence that a Guide can do that on a real call.

# Care desk bulletin, September 2026

Product Operations release `PO-117`, effective September 12, 2026 for Specialist Care. General Care gained Beacon access September 21. This reference is current as of September 24. It is an operational reference, not a learning design.

## When to use this path

Use this path when a customer reports brief whole-home Wi-Fi disconnections involving a WN7 hub or a WNM-7 bundle. Ask for the hub serial in the Northstar app or on the hub. Enter the serial in Orbit's `WN7 eligibility` panel. The tool checks affected assembly batches B24K and B24L. The customer is eligible only when Orbit returns `Eligible` for that serial. The model code alone does not establish eligibility.

If Orbit returns `Not eligible`, continue appropriate troubleshooting or escalation for the reported issue. Do not promise the replacement or free service year. If the serial cannot be located or the tool is unavailable, open an eligibility review case and give the customer a specific follow-up expectation. Do not guess.

## Approved offer for an eligible serial

1. Explain that Northstar identified an issue that can cause intermittent disconnections in some WN7 hubs and will replace the eligible hub at no cost. Do not say every WN7 is affected.
2. Submit a hub replacement order in Orbit. Verify that the order has a confirmation number. The current standard shipping estimate is five to seven business days, subject to the estimate shown on the order. The customer keeps the satellite nodes unless a separate issue is diagnosed.
3. Attach twelve months of Northstar Plus to the household account at no cost. The normal annual price is $149. Verify that the credit transaction says `Applied`. Northstar Plus offers network insights and limited backup connectivity. It is a goodwill benefit, not a repair for the WN7 defect, and it does not prevent every outage.
4. If either transaction fails, keep the recovery case open, document what succeeded and failed, and arrange follow-up through the unresolved recovery queue. Do not tell the customer the full offer is complete.
5. Recap the replacement confirmation, the shipping expectation shown in Orbit, the Plus status, and what happens next. Invite the customer to repeat back any part that is unclear.

## Contact handling notes

There is no known injury or electrical safety issue associated with this defect. Do not lead with that fact when a customer is describing disrupted work or school. Do not ask an eligible customer to repeat generic restart steps as a condition for the approved remedy. Other connectivity causes still exist, including in WN7 households, so do not assume every disconnection is this defect. A completed replacement order with a missing Plus credit is an incomplete recovery, even if the first call was short.

# Connected home products

The WN7 is Northstar Home's midrange whole-home Wi-Fi hub and one of its widely installed network products. The WNM-7 bundle contains the same hub. The affected serial batches are a subset of that installed base, even though they are generating many Care contacts. The hub connects to a household's modem and coordinates the household's Wi-Fi network. Customers may also use two small satellite nodes to cover distant rooms. A household can have work laptops, phones, TVs, and other connected devices on the same network. An intermittent loss of connection is frustrating because it may interrupt work, school, calls, and entertainment. The WN7 is not a safety or medical device. The defect described in this packet has no known injury risk.

A customer can find a model code and serial number in the Northstar Home app or on the bottom of the hub. Guides can verify eligibility in Orbit using the serial number. Customers cannot self-replace the hub through the app. A replacement must be arranged through the recovery path, which produces a confirmation number and shipping estimate. The premium service credit is attached to the customer's account separately. Those are two distinct steps.

Northstar Plus normally costs $149 per year. It provides network insights, device connection history, and limited cellular backup during eligible broadband outages through a compatible hub. The replacement WN7 has the required backup hardware. The free year is a goodwill benefit, not a fix for the intermittent disconnect defect. Do not promise that it prevents all future outages.

Normal troubleshooting may include checking cables, restarting equipment, updating firmware, and reviewing placement of satellite nodes. Those steps are useful for many connectivity problems. A WN7 in an affected serial range with the documented intermittent pattern follows a different recovery path. A Guide should not repeatedly ask a verified eligible customer to retry generic troubleshooting before explaining the approved remedy.

# Northstar Home background

Northstar Home sells connected devices for ordinary households: Wi-Fi hubs, plugs, lighting controls, cameras, speakers, displays, and other home technology. It has more than 240 active product and bundle codes. Its customer promise is "Home tech that gives you one less thing to think about." The company has about 4.2 million active households and a large, hourly Customer Care workforce in three US centers and a remote team.

Northstar calls its customer-care representatives "Guides" and its supervisors "Team Leads." The Customer Care organization uses a cloud call platform named Orbit. It uses an internal knowledge base named Beacon and an LMS named Sana. Sana can deliver short modules, assign cohorts, track completion, and collect simple knowledge checks. The case materials make no assumption that Sana can simulate live calls or assess actual call behavior.

## Internal values in practice

- **Make it clear.** Use plain words and give the customer a next step they can repeat back.
- **Stay with the handoff.** A handoff is complete when the next person or system can act, not when a Guide says it has been sent.
- **Fix the pattern.** When several customers hit the same friction, raise the pattern rather than making each caller carry it alone.

Care leaders refer to the promise as "one less thing." People sometimes use it in customer language, but the brand team cautions against saying it to a frustrated customer before the problem is actually resolved.

## Operating context

Guides handle several product lines on the same shift. Orbit assigns reason codes at the end of the call, often based on the Guide's best guess. Beacon articles are searchable by product code, customer language, and issue tag. A small specialist queue handles escalations and new programs, but most household calls enter general care. Team Leads can review sampled calls and run short huddles. Taking all Guides off the phones at once raises wait times and abandoned calls. Learning can propose and test a response, but Care, Product, Quality, and Workforce Management own their respective operating changes.
